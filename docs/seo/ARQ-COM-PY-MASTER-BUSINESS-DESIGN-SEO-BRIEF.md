**Version:** 1.0

**Market:** Paraguay, initially Asunción and Gran Asunción

## 1. Executive summary

ARQ is a **demand-generation and professional-matching brand for architecture in Paraguay**.

It attracts people who need help with a new house, reform, commercial space, architectural plans, municipal documentation or structural work. It asks a few simple questions, understands the scope, and connects the person with a suitable independent architect or engineering partner.

ARQ is **not an architecture studio**, does not claim to design buildings, and does not present conceptual images as completed client work. Its value is:

1. making it easier for a consumer to understand what professional help they need;
2. qualifying the project before it reaches a professional;
3. matching the project to a verified specialist with relevant experience and current capacity;
4. presenting architecture with a standard high enough that good Paraguayan studios want to participate.

The site must do three jobs simultaneously:

- create desire and trust through an exceptional, publication-quality homepage;
- convert that trust into a structured WhatsApp project enquiry;
- earn non-branded Google traffic through useful service, project-intent, location, professional-profile and guide pages.

The creative thesis is:

> **La sombra es el primer material.**

In Paraguay, shade is not decoration. It is one of the first architectural responses to heat. ARQ's visual language therefore comes from hard midday sun, deep shade, tierra colorada, ordinary Paraguayan brick, board-formed concrete, celosías, galleries and courtyards—not imported luxury imagery.

## 2. What ARQ is—and is not

### ARQ is

- A consumer-facing architecture discovery and matching brand.
- A source of qualified project opportunities for independent architects and engineers.
- A structured intake layer between an uncertain consumer and the right professional.
- An editorial platform for verified professional profiles and useful architecture guidance.
- A Paraguay-specific brand with local climate, language, materials and project realities.

### ARQ is not

- An architecture practice unless that legal and operational model is deliberately created later.
- A construction company.
- The designer or author of the conceptual “Casa ARQ” imagery.
- A marketplace that publishes anybody without verification.
- A directory filled with scraped or invented professional profiles.
- A source of guaranteed project prices, approvals, timeframes or outcomes.

### Brand boundary with Obra.com.py

ARQ covers the architectural and technical project stage:

- needs and project definition;
- architect selection;
- preliminary design and architectural design;
- plans and project documentation;
- municipal documentation when delivered by a qualified partner;
- structural-design matching;
- design coordination or project direction when genuinely offered by a partner.

Construction, crews, materials procurement, contracting and execution belong to the separate `obra.com.py` concept. ARQ must not visually or verbally resemble a contractor: no hard hats, cranes, price-per-square-metre banners or “we build your house” claims.

## 3. The opportunity

The consumer often starts with a situation, not a professional service name:

- “Tengo un terreno y quiero hacer una casa.”
- “Quiero ampliar, pero no sé si necesito planos.”
- “Estoy por comprar un lote y quiero saber qué se puede hacer.”
- “La municipalidad me pide documentación.”
- “Necesito saber si esta estructura se puede modificar.”

The professional market is fragmented. Strong studios may have excellent work but weak search visibility, inconsistent enquiry handling or no system for filtering serious opportunities.

ARQ sits between these two problems. It translates a consumer's situation into a useful brief and routes it to the right independent professional.

The core promise is not “ARQ will design it.” The promise is:

> **Contanos qué querés hacer. Te ayudamos a entender el alcance y te conectamos con el profesional adecuado.**

## 4. Audiences

### 4.1 Primary audience: project owners

Initial profile:

- Located in Asunción or Gran Asunción, with expansion only where partner coverage is real.
- Has a lot, is considering buying one, or has an existing building to reform.
- Needs a house, extension, commercial space, plans or technical advice.
- Values design and professional judgment but may not understand the project process.
- Uses mobile and expects a quick WhatsApp path.

What this visitor must feel:

> “Entienden arquitectura en Paraguay. No me están vendiendo una casa genérica. Puedo explicar mi idea y me van a conectar con alguien serio.”

### 4.2 Secondary audience: architects and engineering partners

Initial profile:

- Independent professional or established studio in Paraguay.
- Has verifiable identity, professional registration where applicable, real portfolio material and defined services.
- Wants suitable project enquiries rather than unfiltered messages.
- Agrees to response-time, attribution, truthfulness and lead-handling standards.

What this visitor must feel:

> “Quiero que mi estudio aparezca con este nivel editorial. Quiero recibir proyectos bien explicados, no contactos sin contexto.”

### 4.3 Future audience: information seekers

People researching costs, steps, permits, land, climate, orientation, plans and professional responsibilities. Useful guides can turn this early-stage traffic into future enquiries without forcing an immediate sales message.

## 6. Business and operating model

### 6.1 Recommended first version

Start as a **manually curated matching service**. Do not automate the matching decision before ARQ has real lead and partner data.

1. Visitor completes the three-step selector.
2. The selection is captured server-side.
3. Visitor continues to WhatsApp with a contextual message.
4. ARQ confirms basic scope, timing and location.
5. ARQ selects a suitable verified partner based on specialty, territory and capacity.
6. Partner accepts or declines within a defined response window.
7. ARQ introduces the parties and tracks the result.

Manual routing initially produces better learning than an opaque algorithm. The eventual routing logic can be built from actual outcomes.

### 6.2 Qualification levels

**Level 1 — homepage selector, minimal friction**

- Project type.
- Lot status.
- Location group.

**Level 2 — WhatsApp qualification**

- New build, reform, documentation or technical consultation.
- Approximate size or scope, if known.
- Desired starting period.
- Current stage: idea, land evaluation, plans, municipal process or work already started.
- Budget range only when operationally useful and phrased respectfully.
- Photos, plans or location pin only after consent and when relevant.

**Level 3 — partner-ready brief**

- Contact details and preferred contact method.
- Concise project summary.
- Location and lot status.
- Requested discipline.
- Timeline.
- Supporting files.
- Source, campaign and landing page.
- Consent and privacy record.

### 6.3 Matching criteria

- Project type and complexity.
- Professional discipline and verified capability.
- Service territory.
- Relevant real work.
- Current capacity.
- Typical project scale.
- Response performance.
- Conflicts or exclusivity rules, if any.
- Consumer preference where multiple suitable options exist.

### 6.4 Partner onboarding requirements

Do not publish a profile until the following are verified:

- Legal name and public professional name.
- Studio name and operating contact details.
- Professional registration or matrícula, where applicable.
- City and genuine service territory.
- Services and project types accepted.
- Real portfolio ownership and permission to publish.
- Accurate captions that identify the actual author/studio.
- Current availability.
- Agreed response standard.
- Commercial arrangement and lead-status reporting.
- Privacy, data handling and attribution agreement.

The empty architect section on launch is preferable to invented content:

> **Estamos incorporando estudios. Los perfiles se publican con matrícula verificada.**

### 6.5 Revenue options requiring a business decision

The original concept does not lock a revenue model. Choose one only after commercial, tax and professional-conduct review:

1. **Fixed fee per accepted qualified opportunity.** Simple attribution, but qualification criteria must be explicit.
2. **Monthly partner membership.** Predictable revenue, but ARQ must avoid selling exclusivity or volume it cannot support.
3. **Territory or service-category partnership.** Valuable later, only after demand is proven.
4. **Sponsored visibility with clear labelling.** Must never override verification or relevance.
5. **Consumer-paid project scoping.** A future premium service, requiring defined deliverables and qualified providers.

Avoid a percentage of professional fees until local legal, tax and professional-ethics implications are reviewed. Never imply that payment guarantees selection or project success.

## 8. Brand idea and visual world

### 8.1 Creative thesis

**La sombra es el primer material.**

The experience begins with the climate rather than with a catalogue of services. The visual world must feel like an architecture publication made in Paraguay, not a property listing, contractor advertisement or international luxury moodboard.

### 8.2 Canonical conceptual building

The visual system uses one fictional, consistent “Casa ARQ” as brand imagery:

- Single-storey L-shaped house.
- Long east-west bar and short south bar around a courtyard.
- Handmade Paraguayan red brick.
- One flat board-formed concrete slab.
- Three-metre north-facing gallery.
- Seven square concrete columns.
- Deep vertical openings with dark lapacho frames.
- Brick celosía at the west end.
- Red-pigmented concrete floor.
- Mango tree, lapachos, cocotero, red laterite and a visible unfinished neighbouring wall.
- Hard 1 p.m. sun and near-black shade.

It is **conceptual brand imagery**. It must never be labelled as an ARQ project, client, completed work or professional portfolio.

### 8.3 Locked interface direction

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Display type | Instrument Serif, weight 400 |
| Body/UI type | Inter Tight |
| Paper base | `#F2EDE4` |
| Warm ink | `#14100E` |
| Brick accent | `#9E3B23` |
| Concrete neutral | `#8C8681` |
| Raised surface | `#FBF8F3` |
| WhatsApp green | Only the WhatsApp glyph; never the button fill |

Typography should be editorial but functional. Display type is reserved for major statements. Body copy stays at approximately 17px/1.65 with comfortable line length. Borders are warm, low-opacity hairlines. Dark sections use restrained grain and image scrims.

### 8.4 Signature graphic device

The celosía pattern is derived from staggered perforated brickwork. It is structural, not ornamental. It may be used only for:

- controlled image reveals;
- the transition from the dark hero to the light body;
- loading state;
- a sparse veil over the architect-partner ribbon.

Do not turn it into a decorative background, gradient motif, star or pseudo-ñandutí graphic.

### 8.5 The one aesthetic risk

The homepage begins with an impossible but believable image: an empty Paraguayan lot carrying the precise shadow of a building that does not yet exist. The future project is first experienced as shade. This is ARQ's memorable device. Everything else should remain disciplined so this moment retains its power.

> **Encontrá el arquitecto adecuado para tu proyecto en Paraguay.**

> **Contanos tu proyecto**

Three short paragraphs explain the philosophy, then the page stops being poetic and becomes practical.

The most important conversion section. It is a three-step selection experience, not a generic form. The user controls recognizable choices and ends in WhatsApp with context already written.

A full-width courtyard or shade image with one raised text panel crossing into the next section.

Heading:

## 10. Mobile experience

The desktop film cannot simply shrink onto a phone.

- Purpose-shot vertical hero image.
- At most two visual stages: impossible shadow, then finished conceptual house.
- No parallax.
- No heavy scroll hijacking.
- Immediate visible CTA.
- Selector controls at least 48px high.
- Sticky header appears after the hero, with one project CTA.
- One floating bottom-right WhatsApp action on every public page; do not duplicate it with multiple floating controls.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` receives a static final hero and immediate content.

The mobile path must still feel designed, but speed and clarity win over the six-stage film.

## 11. SEO strategy

### 11.1 What “perfect SEO” means here

No one can guarantee a number-one result. Google explicitly says there are no secrets that automatically rank a site first. ARQ's objective is a technically clean, crawlable, genuinely useful and authoritative site with strong conversion—not keyword tricks. See Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

The homepage is the **brand and conversion page**. Dedicated pages carry most commercial and informational search intent. This protects the distinctive homepage from SEO copy bloat.

These are strategic seeds, not claimed search-volume data. Validate them in Google Keyword Planner and then refine them with Search Console after launch.

- arquitectos en Paraguay;
- arquitecto para casa;

- proyecto de casa en Paraguay;

- documentación municipal de obra;
- cálculo estructural Paraguay;

**Informational intent**

- cuánto cobra un arquitecto en Paraguay;
- qué incluye un anteproyecto;

### 11.5 Page-template rules

Every indexable commercial page should contain:

- one search intent and one clear H1;
- a plain-language answer near the top;
- who the page is for;
- when that service or professional is needed;
- what information helps scope the project;
- ARQ's role and independent-partner boundary;
- relevant FAQs based on real questions;
- contextual selector/WhatsApp CTA;
- internal links to related project, service, guide and location pages;
- unique title, description and canonical URL.

Do not force a word count. A page should be as complete as the question requires and should contain information written or reviewed by someone competent in that subject.

### 11.6 Content quality and trust

- Name the author or reviewer of technical guides when a real qualified person participates.
- Add reviewed/updated dates only when true.
- Cite current municipal or professional sources where rules or fees are discussed.
- Never copy municipal requirements without checking the applicable city and date.
- Separate design guidance from structural, regulatory and legal conclusions.
- Do not publish generated prices as market facts.
- Do not fabricate “projects completed,” client counts, response times or reviews.

## 12. Structured data

Use JSON-LD only when it accurately represents visible content.

### Homepage

- `Organization` for ARQ with its real legal/operator details, logo, URL and contact points. Google recommends adding applicable real-world and online details rather than invented fields: [Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization).
- `WebSite` with the canonical site identity.

Do **not** mark ARQ as `Architect`, an architecture practice, or a `LocalBusiness` unless its real operating model supports those claims.

### Service and project-intent pages

- `Service` with ARQ as broker/provider only if the relationship is represented accurately.
- `BreadcrumbList` matching visible breadcrumbs.

### Verified architect pages

- `ProfilePage` with a `Person` or `Organization` as the main entity only when the page genuinely focuses on that verified partner and the partner is affiliated with the platform. Follow Google's [ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page).

### Guide pages

- `Article` or `BlogPosting` with real author/reviewer and dates.
- `FAQPage` only when the exact questions and answers are visible; do not assume it will produce a rich result.

### Prohibited schema practices

- No fake `Review` or `AggregateRating`.
- No invented address, price range, credentials or award.
- No schema that contradicts the page.
- No list of nonexistent professionals.

Validate templates with Google's Rich Results Test and inspect rendered URLs in Search Console.

## 13. Google Business Profile decision

ARQ should **not create its own Google Business Profile while it operates as a pure lead-generation company**. Google's current eligibility policy explicitly lists lead-generation agents or companies and online-only brands as ineligible. Eligibility also generally requires in-person customer contact. See [Google Business Profile eligibility](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en).

This has two implications:

1. ARQ's local organic strategy should rely on the website, verified profiles, useful content, partner links, digital PR and legitimate brand citations—not a policy-risk map listing.
2. Independent partner studios should own and manage their own eligible profiles. ARQ may provide guidance only with permission; it should never claim a partner profile.

If ARQ later becomes an eligible staffed, customer-facing operation, eligibility should be reassessed against the current rules before creating a listing.

## 14. Technical SEO and Hostinger implementation

### Recommended stack

- PHP 8.x with server-rendered HTML.
- Vanilla CSS and lightweight vanilla JavaScript.
- Clean routes through `.htaccess` and a small front controller/router.
- No Node build step required on Hostinger.
- All important content and links present in the server response.
- Central configuration for domain, contact data, environment variables and analytics IDs.

### Essential files

```text
public_html/
|-- .htaccess
|-- index.php
|-- 404.php
|-- lead.php
|-- sitemap.php or sitemap.xml
|-- robots.txt
|-- favicon.svg
|-- includes/
|   |-- config.php
|   |-- routes.php
|   |-- content.php
|   |-- helpers.php
|   |-- layout.php
|-- assets/
|   |-- css/site.css
|   |-- js/site.js
|   |-- images/
```

### Crawl and index rules

- Production pages are indexable by default.
- One canonical host and HTTPS redirect.
- Human-readable URLs with trailing-slash consistency.
- Correct `404` status for nonexistent routes.
- XML sitemap contains only canonical, indexable URLs. Google recommends including URLs that should appear in search results: [sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).
- `robots.txt` references the sitemap and does not block CSS, JavaScript or public images needed for rendering.
- No staging/demo language or `noindex` on the real public brand.
- Search Console property, sitemap submission and URL inspection after launch.

### Performance targets

At the 75th percentile of real visits, target:

- LCP at or below 2.5 seconds.
- INP at or below 200 milliseconds.
- CLS at or below 0.1.

These are Google's current “good” Core Web Vitals thresholds: [Core Web Vitals thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds).

ARQ-specific controls:

- Initial hero asset at or below approximately 120KB after AVIF optimisation.
- Preload only the critical shadow/final frames.
- Load intermediate hero frames on scroll intent.
- Below-fold images use AVIF/WebP, explicit dimensions and lazy loading.
- Purpose-made mobile assets rather than oversized desktop crops.
- Subset and preload only necessary font files.
- No heavy animation library unless measurement proves it is needed.
- Static fallback when reduced motion is requested.
- The cinematic hero is cut from six to four or two stages if a mid-range Android cannot meet the performance target.

- The Hostinger ZIP has runtime files at archive root with no wrapper folder or backslash entries.
- The live domain is checked after extraction and cache purge; local success alone is not treated as public launch.
